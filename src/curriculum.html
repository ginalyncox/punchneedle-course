The Loop & The Line · Curriculum

The full curriculum.

Six modules, twenty-two lessons, four projects. Designed to be worked in order — each module assumes the mechanics of the previous one — but every lesson stands alone if you already have the fundamentals.

LevelBeginner → Confident
FormatSelf-directed reference
Estimated time25–40 hours of hands-on work
PrereqsNone — knife-and-fork level dexterity

Module 01 · Foundations

Before touching a needle, understand what a punched loop actually is: a folded end of yarn pushed through backing fabric, held in place by the friction of the surrounding weave. This module reframes the whole craft in physical terms so every later decision has a reason.

  • Lesson 1.1 — The anatomy of a punched loop
  • Lesson 1.2 — Punch needle vs. rug hooking vs. tufting
  • Lesson 1.3 — Why fabric tension is the whole game

  • Lesson 2.1 — Punch needle types and what they're for

Module 03 · Setup & Design Transfer

The unglamorous prep work that decides how the rest of the project feels. Framing without warping the weave, mirroring your design (you punch from the back), and four transfer methods ranked by clarity.

  • Lesson 3.1 — Cutting and taping monks cloth

Module 04 · Core Technique

The mechanical heart of the craft. Grip, angle, punch depth, anchor stitches, row direction, corner turns. Almost every "my loops keep coming out" problem is one of these five variables — this module makes each of them explicit.

  • Lesson 4.1 — The pen grip and the open channel
  • Lesson 4.2 — Threading, tension buffer, and the loose puddle
  • Lesson 4.3 — Anchor stitches: how to start and stop a color
  • Lesson 4.4 — Row spacing, outlines, and filling
  • Lesson 4.5 — Turning corners without pulling loops

→ Read Module 04

Module 05 · Sculpted & Carved Pile

Move from uniform loops to varied pile heights, then shear and carve for dimensional relief. The techniques that push a rug from surface decoration into sculpture.

  • Lesson 5.1 — Setting an adjustable needle for varied pile
  • Lesson 5.2 — Shearing with appliqué scissors
  • Lesson 5.3 — Carving relief, defining edges, and revealing shadow

How a piece survives real life. Tail management, hemming the raw monks cloth border, latex vs. latex-free glue-back, steam-blocking to flatten warps, and mounting for wall or floor.

Four progressive projects

Each project intentionally introduces one new variable so that when something goes wrong, you can identify which variable is off. Do them in order the first time through.

  1. Project 01 · Mug rug coaster. ~5" square. Two colors. Straight rows only. Introduces: grip, anchor stitches, filling.
  2. Project 02 · Simple wall hanging. ~10"×10". Three colors, one curve. Introduces: color changes, curved rows, tail management.
  3. Project 03 · Small floor rug. ~18"×24". Four+ colors, multiple curves, a bordered composition. Introduces: scale, edge binding, blocking.
  4. Project 04 · Sculpted wall piece. ~14"×18". Varied pile heights, carved relief. Introduces: adjustable needle, shearing, sculpting.
